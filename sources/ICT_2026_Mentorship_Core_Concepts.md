# ICT 2026 Mentorship — Core Concepts Extract

> This file extracts what is visible in those notes and does **not** independently validate ICT claims. Numerical/probability claims are labelled as lecture claims.

## 1. Core Learning Sequence

The lecture's stated purpose is **not to give an entry setup**.

**Process:**

`Prepare → Map important levels → Establish context → Identify the draw → Observe delivery → Define confirmation/invalidation → Consider execution`

The repeated emphasis is to understand:

- Where price is likely trying to go.
- Why that destination is plausible.
- What price should do along the way.
- What would invalidate the narrative.
- Entry comes **after** context and delivery are understood.

---

## 2. Economic Calendar = Time Filter

Before analysing price:

- Review the week's economic calendar.
- Note **medium- and high-impact U.S. events**.
- Record the **day and exact time** of releases.
- Treat major events as periods where normal price delivery may be disrupted.

The notes explicitly frame calendar analysis as a **timing/filtering tool**, not as a method of predicting the economic number.

Key question:

> When should the market potentially behave abnormally?

For newer students, some high-impact days may be better used for **observation rather than forced trading**.

---

## 3. Top-Down Chart Preparation

Prepare the chart **before execution**.

### Monthly
Mark:

- Previous month's high.
- Previous month's low.
- Previous month's close.
- Current location within the monthly range.
- Equilibrium / premium / discount.
- Nearby liquidity.

### Weekly
Track:

- Weekly high / low.
- Important weekly reference levels.
- Liquidity.
- Weekly context.

### Daily
Track:

- Previous daily high / low.
- Daily range.
- Liquidity.
- Relevant PD arrays / inefficiencies.

### Lower Timeframes
Track:

- Intraday liquidity.
- Opening gaps / ranges.
- FVGs and related PD arrays.
- Macro timing.
- Actual price delivery.
- Execution only after context is established.

**Sequence:** `Monthly → Weekly → Daily → Lower Timeframes`

---

## 4. Draw on Liquidity (DOL)

The notes repeatedly return to:

> **Where is price going?**

To form a draw, identify:

- Current price location.
- Nearby liquidity.
- Higher-timeframe levels.
- Inefficiencies / PD arrays.
- Gap levels.
- Opposing liquidity.

Then determine the **most logical draw**.

There may be **multiple competing objectives**. The trader's job is not to claim certainty, but to observe which objective price begins to favour.

---

## 5. Pre-Market Range Study

The **7:00–9:00 A.M.** pre-market window is presented as a source of clues for behaviour after the **9:30 A.M. RTH open**.

Example from the notes:

- Pre-market trending.
- Observe post-9:30 delivery.
- Consolidation may follow.

This is explicitly described as a **precursor/clue, not a guaranteed prediction**.

### Record Daily

- Highest pre-market high.
- Lowest pre-market low.
- Relative equal highs.
- Relative equal lows.
- Whether liquidity was:
  - Referred to.
  - Swept.
  - Ignored.
  - Used as a reaction point.
- Time of interaction.
- How long price took to react.
- What happened afterwards.
- Overall market condition: trending or consolidating.

Study chain:

`Liquidity → Time → Reaction → Delivery`

## 6. RTH Opening Gap

### Premium vs Discount Gap

At the 9:30 A.M. RTH open:

- Open **above** previous RTH settlement → **Premium RTH gap**.
- Open **below** previous RTH settlement → **Discount RTH gap**.

The classification is used to create an expectation for how price may interact with the gap.

### 50% / Consequent Encroachment

The midpoint of the RTH gap is treated as an important reference.

**Lecture claim:** the notes state that approximately **70% of the time**, when an opening gap exists, price trades to the gap midpoint within the first 30 minutes (roughly 9:30–10:00 A.M.) in the discussed context.

This should be treated as a **claim from the lecture notes, not independently verified here**.

## 7. Macro Timing

The notes describe a macro as a timing window spanning approximately:

- The **last 10 minutes of an hour**, and
- The **first 10 minutes of the next hour**.

Examples shown:

- 8:50–9:10
- 9:50–10:10
- 10:50–11:10

A macro is **not directional bias**.

Use it as a **timing filter**:

- Direction should already come from broader analysis.
- The macro gives a window to observe whether expected delivery begins.
- If price reaches the macro window but consolidates instead of delivering as expected, confidence in the narrative should weaken.

---

## 8. Confluence, Not Single-Pattern Trading

The lecture's "biggest lesson" is that the model is **not about one FVG, one gap, one liquidity pool, or one macro window**.

The notes combine:

`Time + Price + Liquidity + PD Arrays + Range Structure + Narrative`

The intended progression is:

- Beginner: **Where can I enter?**
- Developing: **Where is price likely going?**
- Advanced: **Why is price likely going there?**

Framework:

`Where is price delivering from? → Where is it likely going? → What PD array / time relationship should facilitate it? → What should price do along the way? → What invalidates the expectation?`

---

## 9. Range Structure and Mathematical Reference Points

On higher timeframes, the notes use objective range subdivisions:

- 50% = equilibrium.
- 25% / 75% = quadrant-style reference areas.
- Further subdivisions can create octants.

The purpose is not decoration; it is to create **specific mathematical reference points** for observing price behaviour.

---

## 10. Monthly and Daily Structure

### Monthly
The notes show:

- Marking prior monthly highs/lows.
- Identifying an **inside monthly candle** when the current month remains inside the prior month's range.
- Treating liquidity above monthly highs and below monthly lows as potential future objectives.
- Asking:
  - Where is current price relative to the prior monthly range?
  - Where is nearby liquidity?
  - Which liquidity is the more plausible draw?

### Daily
The notes show:

- FVGs and Suspension Blocks near current price as reference points.
- Monthly highs/lows carried into the daily chart.
- Relative equal highs/lows around higher-timeframe reference points.
- Daily imbalance/inefficiency structure.
- Range grading using midpoint, quadrants and octants.

## 11. Chart Organisation

The lecture advocates using **separate chart layouts/workspaces** rather than placing every reference on one chart.

Examples shown include:

- Clean / naked price-action view.
- Opening-gap / New Week Opening Gap view.
- Active execution / teaching view.

Purpose:

- Reduce chart overload.
- Keep information organised.
- Preserve clarity when forming the narrative.

## Practical Checklist

### Before the Session
- Review relevant economic events and release times.
- Mark monthly, weekly and daily reference levels.
- Identify nearby liquidity and inefficiencies.
- Define the most plausible draw(s).

### Pre-Market
- Mark 7:00–9:00 A.M. range.
- Record pre-market high/low and relative equal highs/lows.
- Classify market condition.
- Note whether liquidity is swept, ignored or respected.

### At / After 9:30 A.M.
- Classify any RTH gap as premium or discount.
- Mark the gap midpoint.
- Observe delivery during relevant macro windows.
- Ask which competing draw price is beginning to favour.
- Only consider execution if delivery supports the pre-defined narrative.

---

## Core Principle

**Do not hunt isolated setups. Build a narrative from time, price, liquidity, range structure and PD arrays, then observe whether price actually delivers as expected.**
