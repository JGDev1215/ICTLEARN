# ICT Lecture Glossary — Part 1

Plain-English reference for **How New Students Should Start — Part 1**.

**Source basis:** Extracted primarily from the saved final Part 1 lecture reference named above, which synthesises the supplied transcript and five student-note screenshots. A separately saved ICT liquidity-pools transcript is used only where explicitly labelled **L**, to complete the NWOG and NDOG definitions that Part 1 merely names. This glossary does not independently recheck the original recordings. **T** and **L** timestamps are elapsed minutes:seconds; **I** references identify student screenshots. Definitions are paraphrases, not direct quotations. These describe ICT's teaching, not proven market laws.

**How to use:** Find the term in the grouped tables below. Where the reference mentions a term without specifying its complete construction, that limitation is retained rather than supplying an unverified rule.

**Audit result:** The definitions and formulas have been checked against the complete saved Part 1 reference. Version 1.1 separated narrative from directional bias, removed the unsupported use of “raid” as an automatic synonym for “sweep”, narrowed the generic midpoint formula and added omitted chart-preparation terms. Version 1.2 refines NWOG, NDOG and RTH opening-gap boundaries, sources and implementation limits. The original recordings themselves remain outside this audit.

## 2. Ranges, coordinates and midpoints

| Term | Plain-English definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Range anchors | The high and low, or other explicitly selected endpoints, used to measure a particular price interval. Every midpoint or subdivision depends on those endpoints. | T 22:45–23:38; 28:38–29:59 |
| Premium | The upper half of the specified range: price above its midpoint. Always name the range; the same price can occupy different relative positions in different ranges. | T 22:45–23:38 |
| Discount | The lower half of the specified range: price below its midpoint. This label alone is not an instruction to buy. | T 22:45–23:38; source §1 |
| Equilibrium — EQ | The midpoint of the chosen range: `(high + low) / 2`. | T 28:38–29:59; source §4 |
| Range grading | Dividing the chosen range into halves, quarters and eighths to create observation coordinates. These lines do not guarantee a reaction. | T 28:38–29:59; 39:57–40:10 |
| Octant / eighth | One of eight equal divisions of a range. Measurement direction matters: the example's `0.125` line is one eighth down from the high. | T 46:24–46:43; I5 |
| Consequent encroachment — CE | The midpoint of the identified reference. Specify the object being halved: a selected wick or gap, for example. Do not automatically use the midpoint of the whole candle. | Source terminology; T 48:43–49:23; 55:51–56:09 |
| Wick midpoint | Halfway between the endpoints of the selected candle wick. In the bearish example, ICT prefers retracement bodies to remain below the relevant wick midpoint. | T 48:43–49:23; 59:03–60:04 |

Its exact construction is not supplied in the saved reference; do not infer traded volume from the name alone. | T 30:15–31:59; 33:38–34:08; 116:31–117:19 |
| Suspension Block | In the illustrated case, a SIBI with volume imbalances at both boundaries. This is the example-specific description preserved in the reference. | T 30:15–31:59; 33:38–34:08 |
| Immediate rebalance | The label ICT gives the later candle interaction after checking prices and stating that neither an FVG nor a volume imbalance is present there. A universal detection formula is not provided in the saved reference. | T 116:31–117:19 |
| New Week Opening Gap — NWOG | The price interval between the futures closing price at the end of Friday’s session and the opening price when futures reopen on Sunday. For the current standard NQ/MNQ schedule, these anchors correspond to the Friday 17:00 and Sunday 18:00 New York boundaries. Part 1 names NWOG but does not define its endpoints; the close-to-open construction comes from the separately saved ICT liquidity-pools transcript. | T 15:47–16:49; 18:14–20:30; L 25:44–26:32; C1 |
| New Day Opening Gap — NDOG | The price interval between the futures closing price at the 17:00 New York daily close and the opening price when futures reopen at 18:00, normally for the Monday-to-Thursday daily maintenance breaks. The 18:00 reopen starts the next futures trade date. Part 1 names NDOG but does not define its endpoints; the close-to-open construction comes from the separately saved ICT liquidity-pools transcript. | T 18:14–20:30; L 25:44–26:32; C1 |
| First presented fair value gap | A separately maintained FVG reference named in the chart-layout discussion. The saved reference does not establish its qualifying time window or selection rules. | T 43:37–45:12 |

**NWOG/NDOG boundary rule:** These gaps are price intervals between two fixed price anchors, not the high-to-low range traded during the closed hour or weekend. For data implementation, use the last eligible price immediately before the scheduled close and the first eligible opening price when trading resumes, subject to the dataset’s documented bar-timestamp convention. If the two prices are equal, the gap has zero width. Holiday and special-session schedules can change the normal boundaries.

## 4. RTH opening gap and timing

| Term | Plain-English definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Regular trading hours — RTH | The regular-session context referenced in the lecture’s opening-gap discussion, keyed to the 09:30 New York cash open. Nasdaq’s standard cash session is 09:30–16:00, but the lecture does not expressly define the closing-price methodology used for its futures chart. | T 41:41–43:19; source terminology; C3 |
| Previous RTH closing reference / “settlement” | The previous eligible RTH reference price used as one endpoint of the lecture’s opening gap. The lecture calls it “settlement” but does not establish whether this means the official CME settlement, the 16:00 cash close, a futures RTH close or a chart-specific closing value. These must not be treated as interchangeable. CME’s official daily Micro E-mini settlement is calculated from its E-mini counterpart’s 15:59:30–16:00 volume-weighted average price, not automatically the last chart trade. | T 41:41–43:19; 51:28–51:45; C2; C3 |
| RTH opening gap | The price interval between the unchanged previous eligible RTH closing reference `S` and the current session’s 09:30 New York opening price `O`. Its low is `min(O,S)`, its high is `max(O,S)` and its midpoint is `(O+S)/2`. It must not be calculated until both price anchors are available and the meaning of `S` has been fixed. | T 41:41–43:19; 51:28–51:45; 97:16–97:41 |
| Premium gap / gap higher | An opening above the previous RTH closing reference: `O > S`. Here, “premium” compares two session reference prices. | T 42:53–43:19; 51:02–51:58 |
| Discount gap / gap lower | An opening below the previous RTH closing reference: `O < S`. | T 42:53–43:19; 51:02–51:58 |
| Gap midpoint / half-gap fill | The midpoint `(O + S) / 2`; reaching it from the opening side covers half the opening gap. Keep the original anchors fixed when describing that gap. | T 97:16–97:41 |
| Full-gap closure | A return to the previous RTH closing reference `S`. It is distinct from reaching the gap midpoint. | T 97:16–97:41; source §8 |
| Opening-range time window | The 09:30–10:00 time interval discussed in the lecture. It is not the same object as the RTH opening-gap price interval. | T 93:34–93:38; 95:23–95:29 |
| Macro / macro window | The timing window described here: the last ten minutes of an hour and first ten minutes of the next, such as 08:50–09:10. It is used to assess expected delivery, not to choose direction by itself. | T 63:09–63:15; 65:09–65:34; 90:14–90:30 |
| Economic calendar / timing filter | The schedule of relevant releases used to prepare for possible disruption to price delivery. The instruction is to note event days and times, rather than predict the released figures. | T 1:13–2:22; 5:00–5:20; 10:35–11:02 |
| Non-farm payroll — NFP | The employment-release term used to frame the particular week's beginner guidance. The weekday restrictions discussed belong to that teaching context, not a proven universal weekly rule. | T 3:44–4:29; 8:31–9:20; source terminology |

**The 70% statement:** ICT is reported as claiming that, when an opening gap exists, half the gap is filled before 10:00 approximately 70% of the time. The reference supplies no reproducible test or sample. This is neither a verified trade win rate nor a full-gap-fill probability. **[T 97:21–97:41]**

**Implementation guardrails for all three gap types:**

- Use `America/New_York` with historical daylight-saving handling; do not apply a fixed UTC offset.
- Resolve holidays, early closes and other special sessions from the applicable exchange calendar.
- Use the same futures contract and compatible price basis for both endpoints. Do not construct a gap across an uncontrolled contract roll or mix adjusted and raw prices.
- Record whether timestamps label bar opens or closes. For one-minute open-labelled data, the bar beginning at 16:59 supplies a 17:00 close only after that bar is complete; the 18:00 bar supplies the reopening open.
- Do not substitute a provider’s daily OHLC close for an ICT gap endpoint unless the provider’s session and price convention has been verified to match the selected definition.
- Treat midpoint, premium/discount and fill status as measurements of the named gap—not as automatic trade signals or validated probabilities.

## 7. Formula card

Let `H` and `L` be the chosen range high and low, `O` the relevant gap opening price, and `S` the corresponding fixed closing reference. For the RTH opening gap, `O` is the 09:30 opening price.

| Reference | Formula |
|---|---|
| Range midpoint / equilibrium | `(H + L) / 2` |
| One eighth of the range | `(H - L) / 8` |
| First eighth down from the high | `H - (H - L) / 8` |
| First eighth up from the low | `L + (H - L) / 8` |
| Gap low / high | `min(O, S)` / `max(O, S)` |
| Gap midpoint / consequent encroachment | `(O + S) / 2` |
| Full-gap closure level when measured from the opening anchor | `S` |
| Midpoint of an explicitly identified two-price interval | `(price endpoint 1 + price endpoint 2) / 2` |

These are arithmetic restatements of the reference's range measurements, not entry rules. If `O = S`, there is no non-zero opening gap to fill. Always retain the gap type, session date, contract and price-source convention with the calculated levels.

**Illustration only:** If `S = 20,000` and `O = 20,100`, the opening is a premium gap; its midpoint is `20,050`, and full closure is `20,000`.
